# Why Relo exists

**Decentralized agents and automations, together in one place.**

This is the reason for Relo. Every page, feature and piece of copy should make it clearer, not bury it.

## The problem

The owner's work no longer happens on one machine or in one tool:

- **Agents run everywhere.** Claude Code on the laptop, Codex on another computer, a custom
  harness on a home server, something else in the cloud. Each has its own terminal, its own way of
  saying it is done, and its own way of getting stuck waiting for an answer.
- **Automations run everywhere too.** A script that processes files, a scheduled job, a CI
  pipeline, a deploy tool. They finish, fail or need a decision, usually with nobody watching.
- **Apps need a person in the loop.** The owner's own product wants a human decision at the right
  moment: approve a new sign-up, confirm a refund, review a risky request.

Everything is decentralized, so the owner either watches many screens or misses what matters.

## What Relo does

Relo bridges all of it into one place, wherever it runs: a laptop, a home server, the cloud or
inside an app. Every source, whether agent, script or app, can:

1. **Report status.** The work in progress and how far along it is, live on the iPhone's Lock
   Screen and Dynamic Island, or in the Mac's notch.
2. **Break through when it matters.** Time-sensitive alerts for what needs the owner now. Everything
   else stays quiet until the outcome.
3. **Carry the details.** A plan with steps and the specifics, opened on demand.
4. **Ask for input.** Approve, decline, answer a question or pick an option, from the phone or the Mac's notch. The
   source waits for the answer and carries on; no answer means no.

There are two ways in. **Relo for Mac** runs Relo on one Mac: nothing leaves it, and there is no
server, account, iPhone or encryption to set up. Or **a server the owner runs**, with computers and
an optional iPhone, end-to-end encrypted to the owner's devices once an iPhone is trusted.

## Why it matters to the owner

- **Visibility and control as automations take over.** The more work the owner hands to agents and
  automations, the more they need to see it and step in at the right moment. Relo keeps everything
  in view and nothing risky happens without the owner's yes.
- **Efficiency.** Work keeps moving: what is stuck, stale or waiting on the owner shows up before it
  costs hours, and every outcome, finished or failed, is known.
- **Total privacy for confidential work.** Agents work on client code, unreleased plans and personal
  matters, so anything leaving the owner's machines must stay private. With Relo for Mac on its own,
  nothing leaves the Mac. With a server, content is end-to-end encrypted on the producer's computer
  once an iPhone is trusted and opens only on the owner's devices; the server (even the
  owner's own) cannot read it. The server wipes it after the window the owner sets (10 hours by default), and the phone's keys rotate
  every 10 hours, so even an old copy can never be opened again.

Lead with why the owner should care. The technology (end-to-end encryption, signatures,
self-hosting, source availability) is evidence that earns trust, not the headline.

## The three kinds of source

| Source | Examples | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Agents** | Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, Copilot CLI, OpenCode, Amp, custom harnesses | live status of a session's work, questions before risky steps |
| **Scripts and jobs** | processing scripts, cron and scheduled jobs, CI pipelines, deploy tools | progress of a run, the outcome, a go/no-go before a step |
| **Apps and services** | the owner's own product, an auth system, internal tools | a human in the loop: approve a sign-up or a request, by HTTP API with a signed callback |

## How to state it

- Lead with the unification: many machines, many tools, **one place**. Features such as plans,
  sessions or hooks support this; they are not the headline.
- Name all three sources (agents, scripts, apps), not only agents.
- Name the four things the owner gets: status, time-sensitive alerts, details, input.
- One-line forms: "Decentralized agents and automations, together in one place." / "Every agent,
  script and app you run, wherever it runs, in one place on your iPhone or your Mac."
