# Relo's sound language

Relo sounds lowkey, modern and professional: soft tones in one key, in one small room, quieter the more often they
play. A sound tells the owner something they could not see; everything else is silent. This page is the brief every
sound is designed and audited against. The rules live in code in two places: `scripts/sounds/studio.py` (what is
made) and `scripts/sounds/audit.py` (what passes). [ADR 0013](adr/0013-sensory-language.md) maps the sounds onto
Relo's moments.

## Process

1. Variants are generated by `studio.py`, many per moment: one voice, one motif, one register.
2. `audit.py` measures each and cuts every one that breaks a rule below, including earshot's click and truncation
   check.
3. The survivors go on the Notch Sound Bench, where the owner listens and picks. **No sound ships without the
   owner's approval there.**
4. `scripts/sounds/render.sh` ships only approved codes.

## Rules

| # | Rule | Measured as | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One key: E major pentatonic (E, F#, G#, B, C#) | asserted at generation | every note |
| 2 | Low and warm, nothing high-pitched | spectral centroid | ≤ 1.2 kHz |
| 3 | The harsh band stays down | energy share 2 to 5 kHz | ≤ 4% (about 12 dB down) |
| 4 | No fizz or tick | energy share above 6 kHz | ≤ 0.6% |
| 5 | No click at the start | high-frequency share of the first 20 ms | ≤ 4% |
| 6 | Rounded onsets | 10% to 90% rise of the envelope | ≥ 1.5 ms (designed 3 to 12 ms) |
| 7 | Calm transients | crest factor over the sounding part | ≤ 13 dB (UI), 14 (alerts), 16 (heroes) |
| 8 | Starts at once | lead-in before the first audible sample | ≤ 5 ms |
| 9 | Ends in silence | the last 5 ms | ≤ −40 dB of peak; earshot finds no cut tail |
| 10 | Nothing laptops cannot play on small sounds | energy share under 100 Hz | ≤ 15% (micro, transition, status) |
| 11 | Survives a laptop's mono sum | (L+R)/2 energy against L and R | ≥ −4.5 dB |
| 12 | Headroom | true peak | ≤ −2.5 dBTP (rendered at −3) |
| 13 | Pairs mirror each other: same material, opening rises, closing falls | duration, loudness, centroid | within 15%, 1 LU, 25% |
| 14 | One room for everything | a shared small room: RT60 0.15 to 0.3 s; heroes a deep soft hall | by construction |

## Tiers

Loudness is relative to a notification (−28 LUFS over the sounding part); the more often a sound plays, the quieter
and shorter it is.

| Tier | Moments | Level | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | tab switch, section change, switch on/off | −11 dB | ≤ 150 ms |
| Transition | notch, card and window open/close | −7 dB | ≤ 300 ms |
| Status | work started, step finished, answer sent | −4 dB | ≤ 450 ms |
| Notification | done, failed, your turn, linked | 0 dB | ≤ 950 ms |
| Interruptive | needs you (the only one) | +2.5 dB | ≤ 1.1 s |
| Hero | launch, quit | +1 dB | 1.5 s short or a 3 s hall; quit ≤ 2.2 s |

## Grammar

- **Rising** opens, starts or succeeds; **falling** closes or fails. A failure is a gentle fall within the key, never
  a buzzer, a tritone or a minor second.
- **Two equal notes** is your turn; **three, high-low-high** is needs-you.
- **One note** is a micro sound or a step.
- **A pair** (open/close, on/off, expand/collapse) is one material: the closing side is the opening side's contour
  inverted.

## Never

Bells or inharmonic chimes, high ticks, laser beeps, risers, glitch or data bursts, zero-millisecond attacks, cut
tails, long reverb on small sounds, buzzers, mixed keys or rooms, lead-in silence, and the same sound for two
meanings.

## Behaviour

- Sound is never the only channel: every cue has a visual twin, and a haptic one on the iPhone.
- The same cue repeated within 400 ms plays once.
- A burst plays one sound: the highest moment wins (ADR 0013).
- Frequent sounds (tabs, sections, switches) sit on the *interface sounds* switch.

## Sources

- Material Design, [About sound](https://m2.material.io/design/sound/about-sound.html) and [Applying sound to UI](https://m2.material.io/design/sound/applying-sound-to-ui.html): informative, on-brand and reassuring; hero and notification hierarchy; silence for frequent actions.
- Microsoft, [Windows 11 inclusive design](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsexperience/2021/10/04/making-windows-11-the-most-inclusively-designed-version-of-windows-yet/): old sounds were heard as aggressive; the calmer, rounded set.
- Violet Recording, [How to design UI and app sounds](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-ui-sounds/): lengths, onsets, EQ, one key and one patch, intervals for meaning, common mistakes.
- Apple HIG, [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics): audio, visual and tactile feedback agree; each pattern has one meaning.
- Most numeric thresholds are Relo's own synthesis, calibrated on the owner's approved and rejected sounds: the
  approved lowkey sounds pass, and every rejected one fails.
